# Withdrawal strategy worker brief

README: "worstWindow: every start year in the Damodaran data run with fixed-real spending;
the one that fails earliest, else ends lowest." "The engine's four strategies (see the Projection README)."
"Sources: Bengen (1994) and the Trinity study for the 4% rule; Guyton and Klinger (2006) for guardrails; the Bogleheads VPW; the FI Calc guide's definitions."
Rules separately: each of four strategies; expected/real spending min/max/first/end/failure
summaries; worst historical window selection, tie order and insufficient history; chosen/default
strategy and missing data. Obtain source returns from Damodaran, never repo data for expected values.
Use closed-form deterministic/invented sequences to isolate strategies, and small complete history
computations for selector cases. A return-free algebraic invariant alone cannot prove selector choice.
Guardrails are the documented simplified model, strict >1.2 and <0.8 and skip inflation after losses;
test exact thresholds either side, interactions and zero balances. Cite simplified policy explicitly.
VPW end-period PMT vs beginning withdrawal can fail in the final year; preserve disagreement.
Layout common compute and project targets; no full historical-page or Monte Carlo tests.

## Completed independent specification

The eight groups below each contain 60 grid, 30 edge and 10 extreme rows: 800 total.
The four strategy groups replay the pure engine, then the page groups replay the
actual `compute(plan,params)` and exported `worstWindow(inputs)` functions. Tests
of the shared engine isolate one untaxed invested bucket so the spending rules
are not accidentally tests of federal brackets. Page cases include families,
working years, 2026/2056, 98/99-year history bounds, empty lists, missing birth
dates and invalid/missing selected strategy. Money is rounded only on CSV output
to cents, rates to six decimals. Arithmetic inside the oracle is not rounded.

| `fixed-real` | Nominal spending = initial annual spending times price level; price level starts at 1 and multiplies later years' inflation factors. | `guardrails` | Start at planned spending. ### Primary history provenance

The worker downloaded the [NYU Damodaran workbook](https://www.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pc/datasets/histretSP.xls)
directly, independent of every repository return value. Source SHA-256:
`28b8110916a15a4dcc11c87c6422510704608ddedcdfa67a1298abdc22e49c69`.
`tools/golden_retirement/history_source.py` selects the labeled S&P 500,
10-year Treasury and 3-month T.Bill columns, plus CPI percent changes in the
workbook's FRED Inflation Rate sheet divided by 100. It writes the frozen
1928-2025 facts to `tests/golden/retirement/sources/damodaran-1928-2025.csv`.
Six-decimal source rounding is the disclosed history contract; the public HTML
table's two-decimal percentages are too coarse for cent-level replay. Refreshing
the workbook is optional and requires `xlrd`; fixture replay needs no library.

### Independent audit and known disagreement

Every tenth row is asserted a second way (80 checks). Fixed spending and every
historical candidate are checked with discounted liabilities and cumulative
growth, independently of the cash-balance recurrence. Percent uses its closed
growth product. PMT is checked against the algebraic annuity expression and
present value. Guardrail comparisons are inverted into balance thresholds.
The timing identity checks ordinary versus beginning-year annuity values.
Each audited row records its completed check in `note`; failed checks prevent
CSV generation. The lead's separate 2% audit is additional to these checks.

58 timing rows preserve a disagreement: an end-year PMT is removed at the start
of the year. With a $100,000 balance, one remaining year and 3% real return, the
ordinary payment is $103,000 but the available beginning-year amount is $100,000.
The $3,000 shortfall occurs before the return can be earned. The compatibility
VPW rows also expose the resulting final-year failure, despite the page's
"never runs out early" description. The golden key does not alter production math.
