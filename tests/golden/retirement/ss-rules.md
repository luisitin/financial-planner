# Social Security calculation rules and golden data assumptions

## SSA rules and sources

- **Average wage index (AWI):** SSA's complete series is at [National Average Wage Index](https://www.ssa.gov/oact/cola/AWI.html). SSA indexes earnings before the year two years before first eligibility; for retirement at 62, this is the year the worker turns 60. Earnings in that year and later are not indexed. The highest 35 indexed earnings years are summed, divided by 420 months, and rounded down to a whole dollar for AIME. Missing years count as zero.
- **Taxable maximum:** SSA's [Contribution and Benefit Base](https://www.ssa.gov/oact/cola/cbb.html) lists the annual limit; covered earnings above it are capped before indexing and benefit calculation.
- **PIA and bend points:** SSA's [benefit formula](https://www.ssa.gov/oact/cola/piaformula.html) describes the 90% / 32% / 15% formula. Bend points are the amounts for the year the worker turns 62 (based on the AWI two years earlier). PIA is rounded down to the dime.
- **COLA:** SSA's [COLA history](https://www.ssa.gov/oact/cola/colaseries.html) lists annual adjustments; COLAs apply from age 62. The [current COLA page](https://www.ssa.gov/oact/cola/latestCOLA.html) says the 3.6% adjustment for 2026 benefits is effective with December 2026 payments (payable January 2027), so it is not in this October 2026 dollar valuation.
- **Full retirement age (FRA):** SSA's [retirement age chart](https://www.ssa.gov/benefits/retirement/planner/ageincrease.html) lists FRA by birth year. Births 1950–1954: 66; 1955–1959: 66 plus 2 months per later birth year; 1960 and later: 67. A January 1 birthday is treated as the prior year.
- **Early retirement:** SSA's [retirement benefit reduction chart](https://www.ssa.gov/benefits/retirement/planner/1955-delay.html) gives a reduction of 5/9 of 1% per month for the first 36 months before FRA and 5/12 of 1% for each earlier month beyond 36.
- **Delayed retirement:** SSA's [delayed retirement credits](https://www.ssa.gov/benefits/retirement/planner/delayret.html) give 2/3 of 1% per month (8% per year) for birth years 1943 and later, through age 70.
- **Spousal amount:** SSA's [benefits for a spouse](https://www.ssa.gov/benefits/retirement/planner/applying7.html) sets the maximum at 50% of the worker's PIA at the spouse's FRA, with reductions of 25/36 of 1% per month for the first 36 early months and 5/12 of 1% beyond, and no delayed credits. The [quarter of coverage table](https://www.ssa.gov/oact/cola/QC.html) lists the earnings needed for credits; SSA explains [40 credits for retirement eligibility](https://www.ssa.gov/benefits/retirement/planner/credits.html). This fixture assumes a spouse has no own benefit, is insured through the worker, and claims at the same age shown.
- **Survivor amount:** SSA's [survivor benefits overview](https://www.ssa.gov/benefits/survivors/ifyou.html) says eligible survivors may receive 71.5% at age 60, increasing to 100% at survivor FRA; disabled widow(er)s may qualify earlier. A survivor can receive the deceased worker's amount, including delayed credits if earned. The `survivor` column is the worker's benefit amount at that row, a benchmark assuming an eligible survivor at survivor FRA; it is not an estimate for a particular survivor age or family.

## CSV layouts and assumptions

`ss-histories.csv` has 2,000 invented histories: 40 workers with 50 histories each, 200 histories per pattern, and birth years 1950–1995. Earnings are gross fictional annual inputs; each is capped at the SSA taxable maximum for calculation. The ten patterns are steady, rising, late start, gaps, part time, maximum, under 35 work years, too few credits, above maximum, and mixed. Dates use July 1 to avoid the SSA January 1 rule.

`ss-benefits.csv` has 33 rows per history, every 3 months from 62y0m through 70y0m. AIME and PIA use the complete history through 2030; benefit dollars are expressed in 2026 dollars. Missing years count as zero, no earnings after 2030 are projected, and insurance requires 40 credits. `spousal_at_same_age` assumes a zero-own-benefit spouse claims at the same age. `survivor` assumes a qualifying survivor at survivor FRA and uses the worker's benefit at the displayed claim age.

SSA has not published AWI, taxable maximum, bend points, or COLAs for all future years needed by the 1995 birth cohort. For calculations that need future tables, this fixture freezes AWI at the latest published 2025 value ($72,800.33), the taxable maximum at the latest published 2027 value ($192,300), the 2027 bend points ($1,340 and $8,077), the credit threshold at $1,970 for 2028–2030, and COLAs after the known 2026 adjustment at zero. These are reproducible scenario assumptions, not SSA projections. Earnings beyond the 2030 history are omitted, so younger workers' estimates are intentionally incomplete.

All source amounts and benefit rules above come from the linked SSA pages, accessed October 26, 2026. The expected values were generated with a separate Python implementation that reads those SSA tables directly; it does not import the app's `data/ssa.js` or benefit code.
