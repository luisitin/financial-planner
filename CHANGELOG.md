# Changelog

## Unreleased
- [builder] Monthly review (`overview/monthly-review`) is usable: the month in nine steps checked against the plan (fresh balances, the net-worth point, the bank files and filing them, the budget, where the money went, goals, what needs attention, the next step), then "mark reviewed" with a note; new plan section `reviews` (added to older plans on load).
- [builder] How am I doing (`overview/benchmarks`) is usable: net worth against the median family of the same age (the Federal Reserve's 2025 Survey of Consumer Finances, out October 2026), retirement savings against Fidelity's multiple of pay for the age, saving 15%, 3 to 6 months of cash, housing under 28% and debt under 36% of gross income, each as a ruler with its source.
- [builder] Export (`reports/export`) is usable: eleven tables as spreadsheet files (CSV), made on the device: accounts, transactions, income, expenses, debts, goals, holdings, net-worth history, the retirement plan year by year, tax year by year and the life timeline; plus the whole plan as one JSON file.
- [builder] Plan summary (`reports/plan-summary`) is usable: the whole plan as an advisor-style document, opening with a plain-words paragraph, then where you stand, each month's money, retirement with the chance the money lasts, the stress tests, goals, debts, the next ten years, next steps, plan health and the assumptions; "Print or save as PDF" prints the same sheet as a clean document of its own (`modules/reports/report-kit.js`, shared by the reports to come).
- [builder] Next best action (`overview/next-best-action`) is usable: the money left each month after federal and payroll tax, spending and the saving already set up, then the well-known order (bills, starter cushion, employer match, debt at 10%+, full emergency fund, HSA, IRA, middling debt, 401(k), goals, your choice) with what is left on each step and about when the spare money gets it done.
- [builder] Plan health score (`overview/health-score`) is usable: one score out of 100 from seven parts, each scored by its own page (cash cushion, saving, debt, retirement, protection through the stress tests, goals, a complete plan), with where the missing points are and what earns them. F-034 asks the owner whether a goal called "Emergency fund" is the emergency fund.
- [builder] Sensitivity (`scenarios/sensitivity`) is usable: eleven of the plan's numbers (returns, inflation, retirement age, how long it runs, saving, pay rises, Social Security timing and a 77% trust-fund cut, the stock mix, the flat tax) each nudged down and up, ranked in a tornado by how much they move the spending the plan can carry (or what is left at the end).
- [builder] Goal seek (`scenarios/goal-seek`) is usable: set what the plan should leave, and six levers are solved for it one at a time: the most you can spend, the earliest you can retire, the saving it takes, the return it needs, the lump sum it is short (or the room today), and the Social Security claiming age that leaves the most to spend. `copyInputs` moves into `core/math/retirement.js`.
- [builder] Stress tests (`scenarios/stress-tests`) are usable: the plan run through twelve shocks, one at a time: five real market crises (2008, the 2000s, the 1970s, 1929, 2022) replayed as you retire or now, returns 2 points lower for good, six months without the bigger paycheck, never working again (with Social Security disability), the death of either partner (survivor benefits, funeral, life insurance), living to 100, three years in a care home. Each says whether the money lasts, how far below the plan it dips, and the spending it can still carry or the cut that saves it. The retirement engine gains what-if hooks (`events`, `spendingChanges`, calendar bounds on contributions and incomes).
- [builder] Life timeline (`overview/timeline`) is usable: every moment from today to the end of the plan that changes the household's money, in order, on one line (blue while working, green in retirement): retirements, Social Security, Medicare, the ages the rules open things up (catch-up saving at 50 and 60, penalty-free withdrawals at 59½, required withdrawals), debts and the mortgage paid off, goals coming due, children turning 17, 18 and 26, financial independence, savings milestones, and whether the money lasts; a savings chart, topic chips, each moment opening its page. New fixture `qa/fixtures/family-plan.mjs` and flow `qa/flows/seed-family.js` (the sample family made whole for Phase 9).
