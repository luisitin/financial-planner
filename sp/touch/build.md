### What this session changed in the first agent's unfinished work (resume)

- A new rule, T-CANCEL (concern: back): a form a tap opened can be closed without saving it. Checked in both halves. It finds Budget's '+ Add an envelope by hand', whose form has no Cancel while there are no envelopes.
- D-BACK: whether the browser puts the scroll place back on Back hangs on a race between the browser and the page drawing itself again (the shell draws every address from the top). Under heavy load the old 'better of two' gave a different count (one more break in a run with 4 browsers). Now a lost place is tried twice more: lost on all three tries is a break; lost on some is listed as 'Back sometimes loses the place', not counted.
- Three judgments that counted what a person would not call a fault: D-SCROLL-KEPT no longer counts a button that slid down because the part it opened was drawn where the finger was (Documents' 'Write where it is': 93 breaks in the first agent's run); D-ENTER no longer calls a save that dropped a row 'something else'; D-UNDO no longer calls a form's save that changed figures 'a remove' unless its words say remove.

## What a machine could not check
By hand only (`byHandOnly` in qa/touch-rules.json, each with how to check it):
- **H-REAL-KEYBOARD** (input): The keys an iPhone really shows for each box (number pad, decimal pad, email), its Next / Done key, and whether the box and its button stay clear of it. Why not by machine: Chromium has no on-screen keyboard; T-KEYBOARD reads the attributes and D-KEYBOARD-UP pretends 336 px. How: On the phone: tap one money box, one percent box, one date and one text box on Income and Net worth; note the keys and whether Save is reachable without closing the keyboard.
- **H-ZOOM** (focus): The iPhone does not zoom into a box when it is tapped. Why not by machine: Only Safari on iOS zooms; the sweep checks the 16 px that prevents it. How: On the phone: tap a box on Settings and on Get started; the page must not grow.
- **H-WORDS** (input): The words of an error say what to type, in plain words a layperson follows. Why not by machine: A machine sees that words exist and where; not whether they help. How: Read the D-WRONG examples in the sweep's JSON (the 'said' field of each check) and the browser's own messages.
- **H-MOTION** (smooth): The menu, folds and the bottom confirmation move smoothly (no stutter) and nothing flashes when a page redraws after a save. Why not by machine: The sweep measures blocked time and layout shift, not frames as an eye sees them. How: On the phone: open and close the menu, open three folds on Help, save a box on Update my balances; watch for a flash or a stutter.
- **H-THUMB** (touch): The button a page is for sits where a thumb reaches (the lower two thirds of the screen) on its first screen. Why not by machine: Reach depends on the hand; no published line a machine can hold a page to. How: One-handed on the phone: Dashboard, Get started, Income, Net worth.
- **H-SWIPE** (back): The iPhone's swipe from the left edge goes back like the Back button, and pulling down to refresh does not lose what was typed in an open form. Why not by machine: Gestures of Safari itself; the sweep uses history.back(). How: On the phone: type in Add an income without saving, swipe back, come forward; pull to refresh on Net worth.
- **H-RESUME** (focus): Locking the phone or switching apps and coming back keeps the page, the scroll position and what was typed. Why not by machine: Needs a real device's memory pressure and tab reloads. How: On the phone: type in a form, lock for a minute, unlock.
- **H-READ-ALOUD** (navigate): VoiceOver reads each page in a sensible order and announces a save. Why not by machine: The sweep checks names, labels and headings, not the reading itself. How: On the phone with VoiceOver: Dashboard, Income (add one), Settings.

- 5069: one of N like controls in one list
- 3871: in one of N like lists on this page
- 322: opens the same kind of form as one already tried on this page
- 300: opens a further part, two taps deep
- 177: inside one of a long list of like folds that was measured, not opened by a tap
- 150: a link out of the app
- 33: switched off

- Chromium, not Safari: what only an iPhone does (the zoom into a box under 16 px, the real keyboard and its Done key, the swipe from the left edge, Safari's own scroll keeping on Back) is in byHandOnly; the sweep reads the attributes and pretends 336 px of keyboard (the page is not resized, as an iPhone's is not).
- D-WRONG types the year 0026 into date boxes because the owner listed 'an impossible date'. A computer keyboard (or an iPad with one) can type it; an iPhone's date wheel does not offer it, so on the owner's phone these breaks are rare. 179 of D-WRONG's 219 breaks are of this kind (of every kind of break: saved without a word, no words, words off the screen); each says 'a date with a mistyped year' in `measured`, so they can be counted apart.
- D-TIME is this machine's processor time with no slow-down: a floor. A phone is slower, so a page under 200 ms here can be over it there.
- Long lists of like controls are all measured but not all tapped (the first six and the last two of a list of more than eight; of more than three like lists the first two and the last). `--every` taps them all (much longer). They are listed under notChecked with that reason.
- A form two taps deep (a form that a form opens) is not explored: the tap itself is checked; listed under notChecked.
- Links out of the app are measured, not followed (the sweep never reaches the internet); file boxes and date, month and time pickers are measured, not typed into.
- D-BACK: when the place on the page is lost on some of three tries and kept on others (a race between the browser and the page drawing itself again), it is listed as 'Back sometimes loses the place', not counted.
- T-LINK judges a link's words by sharing one meaningful word with the title or the big heading of the page it opens: a line of ours. A link whose words match only the page's search words or purpose is listed as 'another name for the page', not counted.
- The plain-Node half sees no layout: no sizes, no covering, no screenful, no colour. One filled button per card or form there is read from classes (the browser half reads the painted colour). A view that sets its own keyboard by a selector the fake DOM cannot match (a :pseudo-class) would be judged without it; none was found.
- A break's `at` is where the control was when its screen was measured (page coordinates of the screen at that moment); the sweep scrolls every control into view before it taps it.
