## Formulas (compute.js; README "A row for each retirement age (gap #21, 2026-11-13)")
- `ageSpan({ ageNow, planned, answer })`: from max(today's age, 55) to max(70, planned age), widened to take in the card's own
  age (`answer`) when it lies outside (FIRE at 48 starts at 48; an answer of 72 ends at 72). n = to − from + 1 = the title's count
  = the lines.
- The person: the "When ... can retire" card's adult (`workingNow` lead); the first adult counts as stopped from this year when a
  partner is the lead (`withStops`), as on the card. No card (nobody at work, past the planned age, retired couple): no part.
- Each line: the plan with only that person's `retirementAge` = age (Scenario compare's "Retire at age" lever), `buildInputs`,
  strategy `fixed-real`, spending × 1.15 with room for bad luck, one `project()` at expected returns. Lasts = success and
  endBalanceReal ≥ legacy − 0.5 (earliestRetireAge's own test). Runs out at = failYear − birth year; lasts to = endYear − birth
  year.
- Could spend (lines that last): `affordableSpending(inputs)` / 12 for "Just last"; with an amount to leave, the spending card's
  `largestWhere` search ($1 a year) for the most that lasts and leaves it; ÷ 1.15 with room for bad luck; shown rounded down to $10.
- Opt-in: `compute(plan, { ..., ages: true })` adds `ages`; the view asks `{ today, legacy, [spendingScale], only: [], ages: true }`
  (no lever is worked out again). The card's search (`earliestRetireAge`) is worked out once and shared by the card and the rows.
- Sources: no outside figure enters the math. Context for the range's ends, read 2026-11-13: SSA "Delayed Retirement Credits"
  ("The benefit increase stops when you reach age 70"; ssa.gov/benefits/retirement/planner/delayret.html, read via search: the
  page refused a direct fetch); IRS Topic 558 (exception for "Distributions made to you after you separated from service with your
  employer after attainment of age 55"; irs.gov/taxtopics/tc558). Gap spec section 9 lists nothing for #21.
- Speed: 0.1 to 0.6 s per goal on the qa plans (family 0.5 s); never run unless opened or printed.

## Choices where the spec was unclear (Builder chose; README)
1. The rows' person is the card's adult (the working lead), not always the first adult, so test 1 holds for a couple whose first
   adult has stopped; a partner keeps their age, and the part says so.
2. n counts every line, the card's age included ("10 more ages" for 61 to 70, as the design's example).
3. Status "retiring at 55 to 70" (20 characters at most) says what the ages are.
4. The range widens to the card's age when it is below 55 or above 70.
5. "Could spend" only on lines that last (the design's example); with an amount to leave, the spending card's search, because
   `affordableSpending` knows no amount to leave.
6. "as planned" on the planned age; the card's age bold; "could spend $X a month" non-breaking.
7. A line whose plan cannot be built is left out (none on the qa plans).
8. The rows are fetched with `only: []` so the cards are not worked out twice; the result keeps every other field.
9. The page's hero and its lede were left as they are: the opt spec's goal-seek row asks for "the headline per case (L1)", which
   L1 already wrote; the lede is still assembled from two clauses ("As it stands the money" / "With 15% more spending than you
   plan, the money") and is left for the opt commit's review (see HANDOFF 1).
10. "More options" became "Set my own numbers", the vocabulary's title for exact boxes behind chips (R24 forbids "More options").
