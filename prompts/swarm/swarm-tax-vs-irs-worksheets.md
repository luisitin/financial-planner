## The task

You are the **orchestrator**. Each worker owns one piece of Form 1040 for tax years 2024, 2025 and 2026 and builds ten worked examples straight from the IRS instructions, then runs them through `core/math/tax.js`:
1. Tax rate schedules and the standard deduction, including 65+/blind additions and dependents' limited standard deduction.
2. The Qualified Dividends and Capital Gain Tax Worksheet, and Schedule D netting with the $3,000 loss limit and carryover.
3. The Social Security Benefits Worksheet (provisional income, 50% and 85% tiers).
4. The child tax credit and credit for other dependents (Schedule 8812), with phase-outs.
5. Net investment income tax (Form 8960) and additional Medicare tax (Form 8959).
6. The 2025 to 2028 changes from the 2025 tax law: the senior deduction, the SALT cap and its phase-down, the deductions for tips and overtime, and car-loan interest (say which the app models and which it ignores, and what ignoring costs a typical household).

Brief: "Cite the instruction line for every step. Output a table: case, inputs, your result line by line, the engine's result, and the gap."

**Your job:** every agreeing case becomes a test; every gap gets a diagnosis (data, rule, rounding, or out of scope) and a fix as a diff with the test. Put a short "what the engine covers" table in the federal page README.

## How to answer

1. Start with a rationale of at most ten lines: what you changed and why.
2. Give a report in Markdown: findings ranked by impact, each with the file and line, why it matters and the fix. Then the changes for the top findings as unified diffs (or whole files for new ones).
3. A whole file goes in its own fenced code block whose first line is a comment with its path (`// path: modules/x/y/compute.js`).
4. End with the list of files you touched and the commands that verify them (`npm test`, `npm run check`).

Rules:
- Follow the project's rules in `AGENTS.md`: `compute.js` stays pure (no DOM, fetch, storage or timers); money math lives in code, never in an AI model; plain words on screen; every formula gets a test and a written source.
- Never edit `modules/registry.js` (generated by `npm run registry`), `data/` or `planner.properties`.
- Tests use `node:test` and `node:assert/strict`, in the module's `module.test.mjs` or `tests/<topic>.test.mjs`, so that `npm test` and `npm run check` stay green. Say which commands you ran; if you could not run them, say so plainly.
- Every number needs a source (IRS/SSA/CMS publication, statute, paper, or a worked example you show). Put the source next to the number in a comment and in the module README.
- The repo is public: use invented example data only (no real names, addresses, account numbers).
- If something is unclear, state your assumption and carry on; do not stop to ask.
- If you commit or open a pull request, put `Prompt: swarm-tax-vs-irs-worksheets` on its own line in the commit message or pull request description.
