# If history repeated  (`retirement/historical`)

**Status:** usable (phase 5); `heavy: true`
**Purpose:** Your plan replayed from every start year since 1928 with the real returns and inflation of those years.

## Inputs
The projection inputs; `data/returns.js` (generated by `tools/extract-history.py` from `vendor/data/damodaran-histretSP.xls`: S&P 500 with dividends, 10-year Treasuries, 3-month bills, CPI, 1928-2025). `params.quick` skips the safe-spending search.

## Outputs
`windows`, `successRate`, `runs[]` per start year (success, failYear, failAge, endReal, minReal), `worst[]` (5), `best[]` (3), `medianEndReal`, `safeSpending` (the fixed-real spending that survived the six hardest starts), `dataRange`, `verdict`. With `params.perpetual: true` (gap next-60 #53) also `perpetual` (below); without it the result is what it always was, byte for byte (`tests/n1-invariance.test.mjs`).

## Formulas
Only start years with enough years of data for the whole plan (`RETURNS.length - years + 1` windows). Cash earns the bill rate of each year; inflation is that year's CPI change. Safe spending: bisection (`affordableSpending`) over the six worst starts, minimum of those.

**Definition (exact).** For one stretch of N years of history and a share s in stocks (the rest in bonds), the rate w is the
largest share of the starting savings B that can be taken out in the first year, with the same amount raised each year by that
year's prices, such that the real ending balance is at least the real starting balance: end / P(N+1) >= B. Year t = 1..N:
take w x B x P(t) at the start (P(1) = 1, P(t+1) = P(t) x (1 + i(t)), i the year's CPI change), then the year's return
r(t) = s x stocks(t) + (1 - s) x bonds(t). The end in today's dollars is B x G - w x B x H, with
G = prod over t of (1 + r(t)), over P(N+1), and H = sum over t of P(t) x prod over u >= t of (1 + r(u)), over P(N+1).
It is linear in w, so w = max(0, (G - 1) / H) exactly (`perpetualRate`); no search. A balance that went below zero would stay
below (a return only scales it, a later withdrawal deepens it), so an end at or above the start means the money never ran out on
the way (tested). 0 when even taking nothing ends below the start.
- **Prices, a year behind the engine's.** Here year t's amount is raised by the CPI change of the year before it (P(t+1) =
  P(t) x (1 + i(t))), and the end is deflated by all N years' changes, P(N+1): the amount taken in a year is set at its start,
  from the prices the year before ended with. Will the money last? (`core/math/retirement.js`, `priceLevel`) raises a year's
  spending by that same year's change from the second year on, and deflates by the last year's level. Both are sound; they
  differ by one year's lag, so a stretch replayed here and in the engine does not give the same dollars year by year. The plan's
  own rate is the engine's (its first retired year, over the savings then), set beside these rates as a share, not in dollars
  (n1 fix F4: the difference was not written down).
- **Which years:** the plan's years of taking money out: from its retirement year (this year when it is retired already) to its
  last year (`perpetualOf`: 31 years for the family, 2055 to 2085). Every start year history has room for: 1928 to
  2025 - N + 1. The brief said "over the plan's years"; for someone still working that would include the working years, when
  nothing is taken out, so the builder chose the years the money is taken out and says so here.
- **The mix:** the plan's stock share once retired (`retirement.stocksRetired`, 50% unless set), as the engine holds it after
  the retirement year; the cash in the plan is counted at that mix too. No taxes, fees or income: it is about the savings alone.
- **Worst and middle:** worst = the lowest rate (the earliest start year on a tie); middle = the lower middle of the start
  years sorted by rate (index floor((n - 1) / 2)), named by its start year.
- **The plan's own rate** (`planRate`): what Will the money last? takes from savings in its first retired year if markets do as
  well as average (`project(inputs)` with no sequence): spending + one-time and health costs + the year's tax - the year's
  income, floor 0 (How much to take out's `firstYear` `need`, tested equal), over the savings at the start of that year (the
  year before's end; today's balances when retired already). null with no savings then.
- **Hand checks** (`module.test.mjs`): returns 10% then 20%, no inflation: G = 1.32, H = 1.1 x 1.2 + 1.2 = 2.52, w = 0.32 / 2.52 =
  12.698% (with $100: take $12.698, 87.302 x 1.1 = 96.032; take $12.698, 83.333 x 1.2 = 100.000). Prices up 10% in the first year,
  returns 10% and 21%: G = 1.331 / 1.1 = 1.21, H = (1.331 + 1.1 x 1.21) / 1.1 = 2.42, w = 0.21 / 2.42 = 8.678%. On five real
  stretches (1929, 1966, 1974, 2000, 1928) the end is the start to 1e-7 and 0.01% more ends below.
- **Sources:** the idea is Portfolio Charts' "perpetual withdrawal rate" (the withdrawal rate that leaves the inflation-adjusted
  balance where it started; portfoliocharts.com, Withdrawal Rates): the site could not be read this session (the network
  refused it, 2026-11-14), so the definition above is the brief's own, written out exactly and tested by hand rather than taken
  from the site. The returns are the page's own (Damodaran, below). No current figure is used.

**Builder chose:** the status shows the two rates (two figures on the first view, which went from 14 to 2); "Never run the
savings down" for the brief's "the rate that never touches the starting money" (five plain words on a 320 px row, next to "2.5%
