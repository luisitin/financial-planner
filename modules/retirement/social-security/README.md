# Social Security  (`retirement/social-security`)

**Status:** usable (phase 5)
**Purpose:** Your benefit from your ssa.gov earnings record or your statement, and what claiming at each age pays.

## Inputs
`household` (adults, birth dates, retirement ages), `retirement.socialSecurity[personId]` = `{ claimAge, record: [{ year, earnings }], monthlyAtFRA, futureEarnings: "same"|"none" }`. Writes the same.

## Outputs
`people[]` (`fra`, `claimAge`, `source` record/typed/none, `estimate`, `pia`, `monthly`, `yearly`, `options[]` at 62/64/FRA/68/70, `spousalAtSameAge`, `survivor`), `couple` (two adults: `spousal` top-ups, `survivorMonthly` per survivor, `bothMonthly`, `survivorYearly`), `householdYearly`, `hasData`.

## Formulas (the SSA's, tables lifted from ssa.tools via `data/ssa.js`)
- Earnings capped at each year's taxable maximum (a year past the table keeps the last published maximum, as the wage index is frozen at the last published one too); indexed by the average wage index of the year you turn 60 over the index of the earnings year (years after that count as is; future years use the latest index).
- AIME = the best 35 indexed years / 420, floored to the dollar. Bend points for the year you turn 62 = 180 and 1,085 scaled by the wage index of that year minus two over 1977. PIA = 90% to the first bend, 32% to the second, 15% above, floored to the dime; cost-of-living increases apply from the year you turn 62 (published values; each COLA is paid from January of the next year, so the 3.6% COLA of December 2026 is in amounts valued from 2027). Every floor to the dime goes through `floorDime` (x x 10 + 1e-8), so an exact $2,315.50 that binary floating point stores as 2315.4999... is not a dime low.
- Claiming: 5/9 of 1% per month early for the first 36 months, 5/12 of 1% beyond; delayed credits (8% a year for people born 1943 or later) up to 70. Full retirement age from the birth-year table.
- Credits: 4 a year at most, one per the year's earnings-per-credit; 40 needed. Future earnings: the last year repeated until the year before retiring or claiming.
- Spouse (`spousalFactor`, SSA "benefits for a spouse"): half the worker's PIA at the spouse's full retirement age, less 25/36 of 1% a month for the first 36 months early and 5/12 of 1% beyond; no delayed credits. `spousalAtSameAge` assumes a spouse with no benefit of their own, the same age, claiming at the same age. For a couple, each one's top-up is half the partner's PIA less their own, reduced the same way for their own claiming age.
- Survivor (`survivorAmount`): a surviving spouse at their own full retirement age gets the worker's benefit (with any delayed credits), but when the worker claimed early never less than 82.5% of the worker's PIA (the widow(er)'s limit, SSA POMS RS 00615.320). For a couple, whoever outlives the other keeps the larger of their own benefit and the survivor amount on the partner's record; the spousal top-up stops.

## Tables
`data/ssa.js` is generated by `tools/extract-ssa.mjs` from the vendored ssa.tools, plus SSA's October 2026 figures where ssa.tools lacks them: the 2025 average wage index ($72,800.33), the 3.6% COLA effective December 2026, the 2027 taxable maximum ($192,300) and credit amount ($1,970). The last two, and the 2027 bend points ($1,340 and $8,077, derived in code), follow from the 2025 wage index by the statutory formulas; the wage index and the COLA were read on 2026-10-30 in the Society of Actuaries' and news summaries of SSA's 2027 determinations (ssa.gov was not reachable from the session), and agree with `tests/golden/retirement/ss-rules.md`, which read ssa.gov on 2026-10-26.

## Decisions already made (round 4, 2026-11-02)
- `ageNow` = the exact age today, in years and months. `claimed` = setup's answer is "getting" or Income has a Social Security item for the person; `claimedYear` = the year of the claim age. Claimed: the page states the benefit as a fact ("Ruth gets about $2,100 a month, since 2016"), with the figures folded under "Correct these figures"; no claiming-age choice.
- Past 62 and not claimed: `options` run from today's age (rounded up to the month) to 70 (whole ages, the full retirement age and 70 kept), the select offers only those ("now (69 and 5 months)"), and `wait` = { nowAge, nowMonthly, at70Monthly, at70 (the month of the 70th birthday), breakEven }: the age at which waiting has paid more in total, (m70 x 70 - mnow x now) / (m70 - mnow), today's dollars, no discounting (my choice: the plain words a person uses). `pastClaimAge`: the saved claim age has passed without a claim; the page warns.
- Future earnings are hidden once claimed or 70 and over.

## Edge cases
No birth date: nothing computed. A record with under 40 credits: shown as not yet eligible, benefit 0 in the projection. A typed statement figure is taken as the full-age amount.

## Tests
`module.test.mjs`: FRA table, claim factors (62, 65, 67, 70, born 1955), record parsing, credits, the formula on a synthetic record, future earnings, compute on a couple. `tests/golden-social-security.test.mjs`: all 2,000 invented histories x 33 claiming ages (66,000 rows) against an independent key worked from SSA's rules: AIME, PIA, the monthly benefit, the spouse amount and the survivor amount (the key's survivor column is the worker's benefit; the test applies the widow(er)'s limit to it) agree to the dime. One history (`w12-h04-steady-y1957`) is a dime low in the key itself (its generator floored 2,984.80 to 2,984.70); the test allows it.

## Won't get it (review 2026-11-02)
Each person with no figure, no record and no Social Security on Income gets an outlined "<name> won't get Social Security" button, saved as the setup's own `guide.setup.ss[id] = "none"` (what the retirement pages' notice saves), with an Undo. Saving a figure clears an earlier "none". Plan health score and the Timeline send people here, so the answer they name is on this page.

## Review fixes 2026-11-02 (decisions already made)
- Claimed = setup's "getting", or Social Security on Income that is being paid now (going, not a one-off, not ended, not starting later). `paidLine` is that Income line; with no figures here the page says what it pays and that the plan counts it.
- `startedEarly`: claimed while the claim age on file is still to come (disability or survivor benefits before 62). The page says "now", not "since <year>", and the projection counts it from today's age.
- `pastClaimAge` only with figures to claim with, never after "won't get it", and from 70 on a saved 70 is up to date. Someone 70 or over who never claimed reads "can claim now: about $X a month. Waiting adds nothing after 70", with no age picker.
- For someone already paid, the amount box is "What <name> gets a month now" (prefilled); it is stored as the full-age amount that pays it at the claim age (`fraFromMonthly`, as setup does), and setup's own Income line for it follows.
