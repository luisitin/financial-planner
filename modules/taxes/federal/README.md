# Federal estimate  (`taxes/federal`)

**Status:** usable (phase 6)
**Purpose:** This year's federal income tax from what the plan knows and what you type: the bill or refund, your effective and marginal rates.

## Inputs
`household` (filing status, people's birth dates for the age allowance and dependents), `income` (pay, bonuses, pensions, Social Security, self-employment, rent, "other"; items marked not taxable are skipped), `retirement.contributions` (pre-tax 401(k) money comes off wages; IRA and HSA contributions are adjustments), `taxes.thisYear` (figures the plan cannot see: a wages override, other ordinary income, qualified dividends, tax-exempt interest, a Social Security override, other adjustments, withheld, other payments), `taxes.itemized`, `taxes.gains` (from the Capital gains page), `taxes.estimated.paid`, `taxes.year` (null = this year), `taxes.dependentsUnder17` / `otherDependents` / `blind` (null = counted from People), `taxes.hsa.contribution`.

## Outputs
`taxInputs()` (shared by every tax page): `year`, `params` (the year's table, indexed past the last one), `filingStatus`, `inputs` for `federalTax`, `sources[]` (each figure and where it came from), `itemized` (total after caps, lines), `people` (ages65plus, dependents), `gains` (netted). `compute()` adds `result` (`federalTax`: income, taxable Social Security, AGI, deduction used, taxable income, ordinary and gains tax, credits, NIIT, additional Medicare, total, effective rate, bracket, room, balance), `marginal` (the true cost of the next 1,000), `steps[]` (the walk from income to tax), `years`, `isIndexed`.

## Formulas
`core/math/tax.js`, parameter-driven from `data/federal.js` (2024 and 2025 extracted from the vendored UsTaxes project, which is AGPL-3.0, by `tools/extract-tax.mjs`; the figures themselves are the IRS's; 2026 typed from Rev. Proc. 2025-32). Ordinary brackets; standard deduction plus the age/blindness allowance, or itemized when larger (state and local taxes capped, 2025-2029 OBBB cap with the phase-out over 500,000, 30 cents a dollar; on a separate return the cap, its floor and the start are halved, the rate is not; medical over 7.5% of AGI; from 2026 gifts to charity count only above 0.5% of AGI, and itemized deductions lose 2/37 of the smaller of themselves and the income over the 37% bracket's start, IRC §68); from 2026 people who take the standard deduction also deduct up to 1,000 (2,000 joint) of cash gifts (IRC §170(p)); the 2025-2028 senior deduction (6,000 a person 65+, each person's 6,000 cut by 6% of AGI over 75,000/150,000; none on a separate return, FS-2025-03); qualified dividends and long-term gains stacked on top at 0/15/20%; the Social Security benefits worksheet (provisional income = other income + tax-exempt interest + half the benefits; up to 50% taxable between the base and the second threshold, up to 85% above; thresholds not indexed); the child tax credit (2,000 in 2024, 2,200 from 2025; 500 for other dependents; phased out 50 per 1,000 over 200,000/400,000; refundable up to 1,700 a child at 15% of earned income over 2,500); NIIT 3.8% on investment income over 200,000/250,000/125,000; additional Medicare 0.9% on wages over the same thresholds. The gains worksheet's last line: the tax is never more than the regular tax on all of taxable income. Gains netting as Schedule D: short and long offset each other, a net loss takes up to 3,000 (1,500 separately) off any income, wages included (`capitalLoss`, IRC §1211(b)), the rest carries forward. The 3.8% tax counts dividends and interest from the Income page, the typed qualified dividends and the year's net gain or allowed loss (Form 8960 lines 1, 2, 5a); rent is left out because only its gross is known. Years past the last table: brackets, deductions, gains thresholds and the wage base inflated with the plan's inflation and rounded; Social Security thresholds, NIIT/Medicare thresholds and credit phase-outs stay fixed, as in the law.

## Edge cases
Not modelled: AMT, the qualified business income deduction, the earned income and education credits, self-employment tax (self-employment income is taxed as ordinary income with a note), state tax, married filing separately living-together rules for Social Security, the FICA wage base on the additional Medicare tax. Ages use the age reached in the tax year. Dependents without a birth date count as under 17.

Procs. 2023-34, 2024-40, 2025-32; Pub. L. 119-21), each within $1. All 12,000 agree since 2026-10-30 (F-029 fixed the seven causes the key found; RULES.md section 11 keeps a worked example of each). A future disagreement goes into `tests/golden/taxes/federal-todo.json` by cause and runs as a `todo` test.
