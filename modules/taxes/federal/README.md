# Federal estimate  (`taxes/federal`)

**Status:** usable (phase 6)
**Purpose:** This year's federal income tax from what the plan knows and what you type: the bill or refund, your effective and marginal rates.

## Inputs
`household` (filing status, people's birth dates for the age allowance and dependents), `income` (pay, bonuses, pensions, Social Security, self-employment, rent, "other"; items marked not taxable are skipped), `retirement.contributions` (pre-tax 401(k) money comes off wages up to the limit; IRA and HSA contributions are adjustments up to theirs), `taxes.thisYear` (figures the plan cannot see: a wages override, other ordinary income, qualified dividends, tax-exempt interest, a Social Security override, other adjustments, withheld, other payments), `taxes.itemized`, `taxes.gains` (from the Capital gains page), `taxes.estimated.paid`, `taxes.year` (null = this year), `taxes.dependentsUnder17` / `otherDependents` / `blind` (null = counted from People), `taxes.hsa` (`contribution`, `employer`, `coverage`, `coveredId`, `fsa`: the HSA and FSA page).

## Outputs
`taxInputs()` (shared by every tax page): `year`, `params` (the year's table, indexed past the last one), `filingStatus`, `inputs` for `federalTax`, `sources[]` (each figure and where it came from), `itemized` (total after caps, lines), `people` (ages65plus, dependents), `gains` (netted), `pretax401k` (the 401(k) money taken off wages), `k401` (`people[]`: id, name, age, pay, compensation, pretax, roth, limit, catchUp, excluded, inIncome; `excluded`, `inIncome`, `year`), `ira` (`people[]`: traditional, limit, deductible; `deductible`, `over`), `fsa` (election, limit, excluded, over, `offWages`: what came off wages, 0 when wages are typed), `hsa` (`picture`: `hsaPicture` of `retirement/contributions`; `payroll`, `employer`, `excess`, `deducted`: the adjustment, `employerTaxed`: employer money over the limit added to wages), `payTotal`, `wagesTyped`. `compute()` adds `result` (`federalTax`: income, taxable Social Security, AGI, deduction used, taxable income, ordinary and gains tax, credits, NIIT, additional Medicare, total, effective rate, bracket, room, balance), `marginal` (the true cost of the next 1,000), `steps[]` (the walk from income to tax), `years`, `isIndexed`.

## Formulas
`core/math/tax.js`, parameter-driven from `data/federal.js` (2024 and 2025 extracted from the vendored UsTaxes project, which is AGPL-3.0, by `tools/extract-tax.mjs`: only the figures, which are the IRS's (Rev. Proc. 2023-34 for 2024; Rev. Proc. 2024-40 for 2025, with Pub. L. 119-21's standard deduction and $2,200 child credit, Rev. Proc. 2025-32 sec. 3.01); 2026 typed from Rev. Proc. 2025-32 sec. 4, the SSA's wage base and the statute: SALT IRC 164(b)(7), senior deduction 151(f), charity 170(b)(1)(I) and 170(p), the 37% limit 68; the full list is at the top of the tool, read 2026-10-31). The tool applies two rules of law the vendored worksheet figures leave out, and on 2026-10-31 they were applied to `data/federal.js` by hand in the same change because `vendor/` is not in every clone: the child credit phase-out is 400,000 only on a joint return and 200,000 for a qualifying surviving spouse (IRC 24(b)(2)); a separate return has no Social Security base amount, so up to 85% of benefits is taxed from the first dollar (IRC 86(c)(1)(C)(ii), (c)(2)(C)), unless the spouses lived apart all year (`taxes.livedApart`, ticked under Year and people), which takes the single figures. The wage base of a year SSA has published but the tables do not have yet (2027: 192,300) comes from `retirement/social-security/data/ssa.js`. Ordinary brackets; standard deduction plus the age/blindness allowance, or itemized when larger (state and local taxes capped, 2025-2029 OBBB cap with the phase-out over 500,000, 30 cents a dollar; on a separate return the cap, its floor and the start are halved, the rate is not; medical over 7.5% of AGI; from 2026 gifts to charity count only above 0.5% of AGI, and itemized deductions lose 2/37 of the smaller of themselves and the income over the 37% bracket's start, IRC §68); from 2026 people who take the standard deduction also deduct up to 1,000 (2,000 joint) of cash gifts (IRC §170(p)); the 2025-2028 senior deduction (6,000 a person 65+, each person's 6,000 cut by 6% of AGI over 75,000/150,000; none on a separate return, FS-2025-03); qualified dividends and long-term gains stacked on top at 0/15/20%; the Social Security benefits worksheet (provisional income = other income + tax-exempt interest + half the benefits; up to 50% taxable between the base and the second threshold, up to 85% above; thresholds not indexed); the child tax credit (2,000 in 2024, 2,200 from 2025; 500 for other dependents; phased out 50 per 1,000 over 200,000/400,000; refundable up to 1,700 a child at 15% of earned income over 2,500); NIIT 3.8% on investment income over 200,000/250,000/125,000; additional Medicare 0.9% on Medicare wages over the same thresholds (`medicareWages`: pay and bonuses before pre-tax 401(k) saving, W-2 box 5, IRC 3121(v)(1)(A), less the health FSA and the HSA payroll money within the limit, 3121(a)(5)(G); typed wages are taken as they are; the engine falls back to wages when none are given). The gains worksheet's last line: the tax is never more than the regular tax on all of taxable income. Gains netting as Schedule D: short and long offset each other, a net loss takes up to 3,000 (1,500 separately) off any income, wages included (`capitalLoss`, IRC §1211(b)), the rest carries forward. The 3.8% tax counts dividends and interest from the Income page, the typed qualified dividends and the year's net gain or allowed loss (Form 8960 lines 1, 2, 5a); rent is left out because only its gross is known. Years past the last table: brackets, deductions, gains thresholds and the wage base inflated with the plan's inflation and rounded; Social Security thresholds, NIIT/Medicare thresholds and credit phase-outs stay fixed, as in the law.

### Pay and contributions against the year's limits
The limits come from `retirement/contributions` (`limitsFor(year)`: each kind from the newest year published, so a 2027 return has 2027's HSA figures and 2026's others; `catchUpFor`; `hsaPicture`). Each rule was checked against the answer key `tests/golden/limits/` (RULES.md sections 2.2, 3, 4.5, 5, 7, 12): on its 2,900 households of 2025 and 2026 the AGI, the Medicare wages, each person's 401(k) catch-up and excess and each IRA deduction agree.
- **Wages** (when not typed) = pay and bonuses - each person's pre-tax 401(k) money up to that person's limit plus catch-up - the health FSA up to its limit + employer HSA money over the HSA limit.
- **401(k)**: deferrals over the 402(g) limit are pay this year, "to the extent the amount of such deferrals ... exceeds the applicable dollar amount. The preceding sentence shall not apply to the portion of such excess as does not exceed the designated Roth contributions" (IRC 402(g)(1)(A)): Roth 401(k) money counts as the excess first, so pre-tax money comes off wages up to limit + catch-up whatever the Roth money is. The catch-up is the dollar amount for the age reached (`catchUpFor`) but at most "the excess (if any) of (I) the participant's compensation ... over (II) any other elective deferrals" (414(v)(2)(A)(ii)): compensation less min(pre-tax + Roth, the limit). Compensation (`k401.people[].compensation`) is 415(c)(3)'s: the person's pay and bonuses, plus for someone self-employed their earned income (415(c)(3)(B), 401(c)(2)), taken as the Income page's business figure, so a solo 401(k) keeps its catch-up. A row, pay or business income that names no adult is the adult marked "Me"'s.
- **Health FSA** (`taxes.hsa.fsa`): a cafeteria-plan salary reduction is not income (IRC 125; Pub. 969: "You don't pay federal income tax or employment taxes on the salary you contribute ... to the FSA") nor Medicare wages (3121(a)(5)(G)); excluded up to the year's limit (125(i): 3,300 in 2025, Rev. Proc. 2024-40 s.2.16; 3,400 in 2026, Rev. Proc. 2025-32 s.4.15), the part over stays in pay (RULES.md convention 8), with a note.
- **HSA** (payroll money = the HSA rows' amounts, or the HSA page's contribution when larger; `hsaPicture`): excluded only "to the extent such amounts do not exceed the limitation under section 223(b)" (106(d)(1)); with no high-deductible plan all of it is pay (223(c)(1)); "Excess contributions made by your employer are included in your gross income" (Pub. 969). Adjustment = payroll money - the household's excess (at least 0); employer money over the limit beyond that is added to wages. All HSA money is taken to go in through pay, as the HSA page assumes.
- **Medicare wages** (the 0.9% tax) = pay before pre-tax 401(k) saving (3121(v)(1)(A)) - the FSA excluded - the HSA payroll money + the HSA excess: cafeteria-plan payments are not wages (3121(a)(5)(G); Pub. 15-B: HSA money is free of Medicare tax "Up to specified dollar limits").
- **Traditional IRA**: deducted (still assumed deductible) up to each person's IRA limit plus catch-up, "shall not exceed the lesser of (A) the deductible amount, or (B) ... compensation" (219(b)(1); limit and catch-up 219(b)(5)); the part over is not deducted, with a note.
- **Typed wages** are the household's W-2 box 1 and are used as they are: nothing is taken off or added, and Medicare wages are the typed figure.

### Married filing separately: two returns
Since 2026-10-31 a separate filing with two adults is two returns, one per spouse ("If you file a separate return, you generally report only your own income, credits, and deductions", Publication 501 (2025), Married Filing Separately, read 2026-10-31). `taxInputs` builds `separate`, one set of inputs per spouse; `core/math/tax.js` (`separateTax`) works each return on its own and adds them up, each return's tax to the cent.
- **Each spouse's own:** their pay and bonuses less their own pre-tax 401(k) money (limits per person, as above), their IRA deduction, their HSA money, and every Income row in their name (business, rental, pension, investment, Social Security). Their own age (65+ allowance) and blindness (a count of 2 puts one on each return).
- **In neither name:** an Income row without an adult is split in half, except pay, which is the adult marked "Me"'s (as above). The figures typed on this page (other income, qualified dividends, adjustments, Social Security, tax-exempt interest, withholding, payments) and the itemized amounts are halved, since the plan does not record whose they are. The sales are halved too, so each return nets its half and has its own 1,500 loss limit (IRC 1211(b)). Typed wages are shared in proportion to each spouse's pay (half each with no pay on file). The health FSA stays with the adult marked "Me".
- **Children** go on the return of the spouse with the larger pay (the Which way to file page's convention); only one return can claim a child.
- **Itemizing:** if one return itemizes, both must (IRC 63(c)(6)(A)): the other return is worked again with its own itemized amount, however small.
- **The next dollar and the bracket** shown on the page are those of the return of the adult marked "Me" (`inputs.separateTo`): an amount another page adds to the household's figures (a raise, a gift, a sale, the health FSA) lands on that return, and an amount it takes off comes off that return. A page that changes one spouse's own pay or saving re-runs `taxInputs` on a changed plan instead (the HSA page does).
- The hero and the stats show the two returns added; the "Two returns" card shows each one. The Social Security rule for a separate return (above) applies to each return.

## Edge cases
Not modelled: AMT, the qualified business income deduction, the earned income and education credits, self-employment tax (self-employment income is taxed as ordinary income with a note), state tax, the FICA wage base on the additional Medicare tax. Ages use the age reached in the tax year. Dependents without a birth date count as under 17.
