# HSA and FSA  (`taxes/hsa-fsa`)

**Status:** usable (phase 6)
**Purpose:** The health savings account: its limit, the room left, the tax it saves, what it grows into; and the FSA use-it-or-lose-it deadline.

## Inputs
`taxes.hsa` (`{ coverage: "self"|"family"|"none", coveredId?, contribution, employer, fsa, fsaPartner?, fsaSpent }`, edited here; `coverage` left out reads as self-only, the old default; `coveredId` is asked when there are two adults and saved for self-only cover), the HSA rows on the Contributions page (each adult's own money from pay and the employer's), `household` (ages for the 55+ catch-up and the years to 65, filing status for the shared family limit), `income` (each adult's pay, for the payroll tax), `accounts` of type `hsa` (today's balance), `assumptions` and `retirement.stocksNow` (growth rate), everything the Federal estimate reads (for the tax saved).

## Outputs
`hsa`: coverage, coverageChosen, coveredId, base (the cover's amount), catchUpAmount, catchUpAge, limit (household: the cover's amount + the catch-ups of the adults it covers), catchUp, contribution (the household's own money from pay), employer, total, room, over, excess (money taxed as pay), deducted (money kept out of income), taxSaved (the federal return without the money less with it), ficaSaved, socialSecuritySaved, medicareSaved, savedTotal, marginal (taxSaved / deducted), years to 65, growthRate, kept (money within the limit), futureValue of that amount repeated, balance, balanceGrown, atSixtyFive, aboveWageBase (names), `people` (per adult: eligible, catchUp, limit, payroll, employer, used, room, over, excess, pay, ficaWith, ficaWithout, socialSecuritySaved, medicareSaved, ficaSaved, aboveWageBase). `fsa` (election, limit, spent, unspent, daysLeft, carryover, taxSaved, over), `wageBase`, `limitsYear` (the HSA figures' year), `fsaYear`, `notes`.

Also exported: `payrollTaxSaved(without, withIt, wageBase)` and `withoutHsaPayroll(plan)`.

## Formulas
- **Limits** from `modules/retirement/contributions/data/limits.js`, read through `limitsFor(year)`: each kind from the newest year that has it, so a 2027 date has 2027's HSA limits ($4,500 self-only, $9,000 family; Rev. Proc. 2026-24 s.3.01(1), read 2026-10-31) and 2026's FSA ones. HSA: Rev. Proc. 2024-25 (2025) / 2025-19 (2026); FSA: Rev. Proc. 2024-40 / 2025-32.
- **Who may put money in, and the limit**: `hsaPicture(plan, year)` on the Contributions page (its README has every rule). Without a high-deductible plan (`coverage: "none"`) nobody may: the limit is 0 and all the money is over (IRC 223(b)(1), (c)(1)(A); https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/223, read 2026-10-31). Self-only cover: only the covered adult (`coveredId`, else the adult marked "Me"), so only that adult's $1,000 catch-up from 55 counts: "Each spouse must make the additional contribution to their own HSA" (Pub. 969; 223(b)(3)(A); https://www.irs.gov/publications/p969, read 2026-10-31). Family cover: every adult; married spouses share one family limit (223(b)(5)). The household limit = the cover's amount + the catch-ups of the covered adults 55 and over; room and over against all the HSA money, the employer's included (223(b)(4)(B)).
- **Income tax saved** = the Federal estimate's total tax on a copy of the plan without the household's own HSA money from pay (`withoutHsaPayroll`: the HSA rows' amounts and this page's contribution set to 0, the employer's money kept; typed wages, which are W-2 box 1 and so already leave the money out, are raised by the own money kept out of income plus any employer excess its absence brings) less its tax on the plan itself. Without the money it is back in wages and in Medicare wages, so the 0.9% additional Medicare tax above $200,000 of wages ($250,000 joint) is in the saving (3121(a)(5)(G), 3101(b)(2); Form 8959 instructions; https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i8959.pdf, read 2026-10-31). Only money within the limit is kept out of income (106(d)(1); Pub. 969 "Excess contributions made by your employer are included in your gross income"), and the excess is worked again on the employer's money alone. The Federal estimate also takes the health FSA off wages (125; Pub. 969), counts pre-tax 401(k) money over its limit as income (402(g)(1)(A)) and deducts a traditional IRA only up to its limit (219(b)(1)), so the saving is worked at the right rate (see its README). Filing separately, the Federal estimate works two returns, one each (its README), so the saving lands on the return of the person whose pay the money comes from.
- **Payroll tax saved**, per adult: payroll-tax wages = pay (Income page) - the adult's own health FSA within its limit (`fsa` is the adult marked "Me"'s, `fsaPartner` the other adult's; with only `fsa` typed this is the answer key's convention, the FSA through A's employer) - the adult's own HSA money from pay + the adult's HSA money over the limit; 401(k) money stays in (3121(v)(1)(A)). Without the money: pay - the FSA + the excess of the employer's money alone. Saved = 6.2% x (min(base, without) - min(base, with)) + 1.45% x (without - with), each from 0 up (3101(a), (b)(1); 3121(a)(1): no Social Security tax on pay above the base). HSA money from pay is free of payroll tax only up to the limit (Pub. 15-B (2026) "Up to specified dollar limits, cash contributions to the HSA ... are exempt from ... social security tax, Medicare tax"; https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15b, read 2026-10-31). The money is the account owner's (the Contributions row's person, else the account's owner); the HSA page's typed figures where they are more than the rows go on the covered adult (or "Me"), as in tests/golden/limits/RULES.md 5.4 and 6.2. The base is `maximumEarnings(year)` (modules/retirement/social-security/compute.js: $176,100 in 2025, $184,500 in 2026, $192,300 in 2027; SSA, 89 FR 85276, 90 FR 49047, 91 FR 67380). With no pay on the Income page the page cannot tell where pay sits against the base, so the money within the limit saves the full 7.65%.
- **FSA tax saved**: each employee elects their own health FSA, up to the limit each (IRC 125(i); Notice 2012-40: "each spouse may elect to make salary reduction contributions of up to $2,500 (as indexed for inflation) to his or her health FSA, even if both participate in the same health FSA sponsored by the same employer", https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-12-40.pdf, read 2026-10-31). Income tax saved = the Federal estimate's total tax on a copy of the plan with no FSA (typed wages, which are box 1, raised by the FSA money kept out) less its tax on the plan; payroll tax saved = for each adult, `payrollTaxSaved` on their payroll-tax wages with and without their own FSA (within the limit), so pay above the Social Security limit saves only the 1.45% (7.65% of the money when no pay is on file). Until 2026-10-31 the FSA was one household election on the adult marked "Me", capped at one limit, and its payroll saving was a flat 7.65% of the whole election.
- **Growth**: the plan's blended expected return, future value of a level yearly amount (this year's money within the limit, `kept` = total - excess: the excess has to come out), to 65 of the covered adult (self-only) or the adult marked "Me".

## Edge cases
No high-deductible plan with money going in: a warning that only someone covered by one can put money in, and that it is taxed as pay. A spouse outside a self-only plan with HSA money: a warning naming them. A spouse 55 or over who does not use the catch-up cannot hand it to the other: a warning when that leaves more over than the household figure shows. A general-purpose FSA and an HSA in the same year get a warning (only a limited-purpose FSA is allowed). Over-contributions are flagged (6% excise). Pay above the Social Security pay limit: a line says the HSA money from it saves only the 1.45% Medicare tax. Medicare is text only: no HSA money from the first month of Medicare entitlement (IRC 223(b)(7)); Part A is backdated up to six months, not before 65, when someone applies after 65 (medicare.gov, "Working past 65"; read 2026-10-31).

## Which return it reads (round 8, batch 1 follow-up)

**(a) This household's own tax year.** The tax the HSA and the health FSA save is the household's own: the year's return with the money against without it. Source: the same function the Federal estimate uses, `known()` in `modules/taxes/federal/known.js` (through `taxView()`), so this page and that one quote one marginal rate, one AGI and one tax. Both returns (with and without the HSA/FSA money) are worked by `taxView()`, so the freelancer's business, the widow's withdrawals and the rentals are in both; the saving per dollar is the Federal estimate's next-dollar cost (9.6 cents for a freelancer whose 20% business deduction is capped at 20% of taxable income). A self-employed person with no pay on file saves no payroll tax on HSA money (it goes in outside a cafeteria plan and does not lower self-employment income). The page keeps the IRA deduction as the plan assumes (`without: "ira"`); on a return where the HSA lowers the IRA's modified AGI the Federal estimate's own returns would show a little more (owner question).

## Round 8 follow-up: Medicare, wages, the real return (decided 2026-11-07; the owner may change any of it)
- **Medicare ends HSA saving.** A person 65 or older who receives Social Security (an Income-page item of type Social Security that begins no later than June of the next year, since Part A goes back six months, a one-time payment such as the $255 death payment and a dependent's item not counted; with no item of their own, Get started's "I get it now" saved with no amount, `guide.setup.ss[id] === "getting"`, as the Social Security page reads it; else Social Security typed on the Federal estimate page, which counts when there is one adult, or when a 65+ adult has a spouse under 62; with two adults and no way to tell whose it is, nothing is inferred and the page asks) is treated as enrolled in Medicare Part A: Part A is automatic for people getting Social Security benefits (medicare.gov "How do I sign up for Medicare", read 2026-11-06) and starts the month they turn 65, or the month before when the birthday is the 1st (medicare.gov "When does Medicare coverage start", read 2026-11-06), or, when Social Security begins after 65, six months before the month it begins (medicare.gov "When does Medicare coverage start", read 2026-11-07: "Your Part A coverage starts 6 months back from when you sign up or when you apply for benefits from Social Security ... Coverage can't start earlier than the month you turned 65"; Pub. 969 calls HSA money in a period of retroactive Medicare coverage excess). The page assumes the person applied in the month benefits begin; applying earlier moves the month earlier. The note says the six-month backdating only when a Social Security start after 65 sets the month (`backdatedFrom` of `adultMedicare`), and says the limit by months ("Only the 6 months before July count toward this year's HSA limit"), never that money added from that month on is not allowed: the year's prorated limit can be paid in any time until the April filing deadline (Pub. 969's example: 2,650 for someone on Medicare from July). The catch-up note ("the extra 1,000 from age 55 can go only into that person's own HSA") is not shown when the excess is a Medicare spouse's. From that month the HSA limit is 0 (IRC 223(b)(7); IRS Pub. 969, read 2026-11-06: "Beginning with the first month you are enrolled in Medicare, your contribution limit is zero"); in the year they turn 65 it is prorated by month (Pub. 969's example: 5,300 x 6 / 12). Code: `core/math/medicare.js` (`medicareFrom`), `modules/taxes/federal/hsa-medicare.js` (`hsaPictureMedicare`, used by the Federal estimate so its return taxes the excess). The page says: "Once you're on Medicare you can't add to an HSA; you can still spend what is in it on medical bills, tax-free", and a typed contribution shows as too much with what to do (take it out before the filing deadline, or stop adding). **No new field.** Someone 65+ who has no Social Security on file is not assumed to be on Medicare (they may still work and be covered; delaying benefits means no automatic enrollment). Part B is not automatic, but the HSA bar follows Part A. Married with family cover and one spouse on Medicare part of the year (Form 8889 lines 6 and 7; IRC 223(b)(5)): the Medicare spouse's own part of the family limit is at most the family limit x months eligible / 12 and their catch-up is $1,000 x months eligible / 12; together the two can use the family limit for the months either is eligible; money past that is divided as near equal halves as each one's money allows, and the rest is excess on whoever put in more. A spouse on Medicare all year takes no part and the other keeps the whole family limit and their own catch-up (the married rules apply only when both spouses are eligible, Pub. 969). Not modelled: Medicare from disability under 65, someone who applied for Social Security earlier than the month benefits begin. Money that did not come out of pay (a retiree's own) is not called "taxed as pay": it gets no tax break and is charged 6% a year.
- **No wages, no payroll tax.** HSA and FSA money saves Social Security and Medicare tax only when it comes out of pay through an employer's cafeteria plan (IRS Pub. 15-B and Pub. 969, read 2026-11-06). With no pay on file for anyone, no typed wages, and other income on the Income page (a pension, Social Security, a business), the page saves 0 payroll tax. A plan with nothing on the Income page keeps the old convention (the full 7.65%). The same holds for a health FSA: it comes out of pay through an employer, so with no pay on file it saves no payroll tax (a note says so).
- **Q1 decided: the real return.** The page (`view.js` passes `iraAsAssumed: false`) no longer forces the plan's assumed IRA deduction: it works the saving on the Federal estimate's own return with and without the HSA money, so an HSA that also frees up IRA deductibility (the IRA phase-out uses modified AGI, IRC 219(g)(3)) shows the whole saving (couple-home reworked: 28.4 cents a dollar, was 22).
  `compute()` called without that parameter still keeps the plan's assumed IRA deduction, because the frozen answer key (tests/golden/limits, taxsaved) counts the saving so; the key should be regenerated on the real return when the owner agrees (reports/tax and the tests call it that way).
